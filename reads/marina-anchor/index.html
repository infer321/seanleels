MARINA and ANCHOR: Lucentis for wet AMD

Monthly Lucentis (ranibizumab) changed neovascular AMD care from slowing vision loss to improving average vision. In MARINA, 94.6% avoided a 3-line loss at 12 months vs 62.2% with sham. Controls were not untreated: both trials allowed PDT.

Study snapshot

Design
Two phase III, double-masked, randomized trials published together. Both were superiority trials with a 12-month primary outcome and a 2-year follow-up
MARINA
716 patients with minimally classic or occult (no classic) choroidal neovascularization (CNV). Monthly Lucentis 0.3 or 0.5 mg vs sham injections, for 24 months
ANCHOR
423 patients with predominantly classic CNV, randomized 1:1:1. Monthly Lucentis 0.3 or 0.5 mg plus sham PDT vs monthly sham injections plus active verteporfin PDT. PDT retreatment was assessed every 3 months
Primary outcome
Proportion losing fewer than 15 letters from baseline at 12 months (avoiding a 3-line loss)
Funding
Not verified

Effect

All comparisons favored Lucentis (P < .001), and the 0.3 mg doses did nearly as well as 0.5 mg. The result was larger than preserving vision: average acuity improved, which had not been shown with earlier treatments. In MARINA the gain held at 24 months.

Safety

Presumed endophthalmitis occurred in 5 patients (1.0%) given Lucentis over 24 months in MARINA, and in 2 of 140 (1.4%) given 0.5 mg over the first year in ANCHOR. In the 2-year ANCHOR report, 3 of 277 (1.1%) pooled Lucentis patients developed presumed endophthalmitis, or about 0.05% per injection. These are patient-level rates over different periods, so the trials cannot be compared directly, and they were too small to exclude rare systemic harms.

What this was not

It was not a cure: the benefit depended on continued monthly treatment. It did not compare anti-VEGF drugs or test other regimens such as as-needed or treat-and-extend dosing. It concerned neovascular AMD, not drusen or geographic atrophy. AREDS2 supplements do not replace injections for active wet AMD.

How far to trust it

Highfor the 12-month visual benefit with fixed monthly dosing

Two large, double-masked trials with the same direction and size of effect, a clear primary outcome and little doubt about the vision result. Most open questions concern regimen, drug choice and long-term safety, not whether anti-VEGF works.

What I'd change

Test as-needed and treat-and-extend regimens against fixed monthly dosing, compare anti-VEGF drugs head to head (CATT and IVAN did this), and follow patients longer for atrophy and systemic safety. Report patient-reported function as a prespecified primary outcome.

Clinical significance takeaway

For active neovascular AMD, arrange prompt intravitreal anti-VEGF. Counsel that repeated injections and follow-up are usually needed, with the regimen adjusted to the agent, OCT findings and visual response.

Bottom line

MARINA and ANCHOR 2006: for neovascular AMD, monthly Lucentis (ranibizumab) beat sham and verteporfin PDT (94.6% vs 62.2% avoided a 3-line loss in MARINA); start anti-VEGF promptly and expect ongoing injections, otherwise vision usually keeps falling.
