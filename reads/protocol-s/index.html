Protocol S: ranibizumab vs PRP for proliferative diabetic retinopathy

Strong evidence for injections as an alternative to laser. In practice the trial compared starting with injections against starting with laser plus injections for DME.

Study snapshot

Design
Randomized noninferiority trial, DRCR Retina Network, 55 US sites
Population
Adults with PDR, no prior PRP, vision 20/320 or better; DME allowed (22% and 23% at baseline)
N
305 participants, 394 eyes (89 with one eye in each arm)
Intervention
Ranibizumab 0.5 mg every 4 weeks, then guided by neovascularization; PRP only for failure
Comparator
PRP, with ranibizumab allowed for DME
Primary outcome
Mean change in visual acuity at 2 years; noninferiority margin 5 letters
Follow-up
2 years (primary); 5-year results reported in 2018
Funding
National Institutes of Health; study drug supplied by the trial
Registration
NCT01489189

What they found

Protocol S, 2 years

Injections preserved vision as well as laser: vision improved +2.8 letters with injections vs +0.2 with laser (difference +2.2, 95% CI −0.5 to +5.0), which met noninferiority. Final vision was not proven better with either on the primary analysis.

Vision averaged across the 2 years (area under the curve) favored injections, with less peripheral vision loss. In eyes without DME at the start, new vision-impairing DME developed in 9% with injections vs 28% with laser. Vitrectomy was 4% vs 15%.

53% of laser eyes also got injections for DME, and 6% of injection eyes needed rescue PRP. 87% of surviving participants completed the 2-year visit.

Protocol S, 5 years · JAMA Ophthalmol 2018

Average final vision was the same, about 20/25 in both groups (difference 0.6 letters, 95% CI −2.3 to 3.5). New vision-impairing DME: 22% with injections vs 38% with laser (cumulative probability). Vitreous hemorrhage was common in both, with no significant difference (58% vs 54%).

Injection eyes averaged 19 injections vs 5 in laser eyes, so laser-first was not injection-free. Only 66% of surviving participants (61% of eyes) completed the 5-year visit. Good vision with either strategy, injections often still needed, and a lot of missing patients.

CLARITY, 1 year · Lancet 2017

Aflibercept vs PRP. Injections gave statistically better vision: 3.9 letters better than PRP (95% CI 2.3 to 5.6), less than one chart line. Included treatment-naive eyes (53%) and eyes with active PDR despite prior laser (47%); excluded DME at baseline. Average 4.4 injections in 1 year, but injection patients were still seen every 4 weeks, vs every 8 weeks for laser. 65% of laser patients needed more laser. Vitreous hemorrhage was 9% with injections vs 18% with laser. 91% had 1-year vision measurements.

How far to trust it

High for the 2-year result

Strong, because it was randomized, with concealed allocation, masked vision graders and high retention.

CLARITY removed some extremely poor vision readings due to hemorrhage, but not every hemorrhage outcome. Bleeding-related vision loss matters. It is also only 1-year outcomes, not lifetime.

CLARITY cannot be compared directly against Protocol S: aflibercept vs ranibizumab, different patients, different follow-up. Apples vs oranges.

What I'd change

Separate eyes with DME, so the trial compares anti-VEGF alone with PRP alone, and add longer-term follow-up.

Clinical significance takeaway

I'd first assess whether this is high-risk PDR, obtain OCT for DME, and look for hemorrhage or traction. For uncomplicated PDR without vision-impairing DME, I'd favor PRP if sustained follow-up is uncertain. If follow-up is reliable, I'd discuss either PRP or anti-VEGF. With vision-impairing center-involved DME, I'd favor anti-VEGF and consider adding PRP.

Bottom line

Protocol S 2015: for PDR, ranibizumab is noninferior to PRP at 2 years with less DME (9% vs 28%); anti-VEGF if DME, PRP if follow-up is unreliable.
