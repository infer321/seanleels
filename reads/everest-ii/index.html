EVEREST II: PDT plus Lucentis for polypoidal choroidal vasculopathy

In symptomatic polypoidal choroidal vasculopathy (PCV), adding verteporfin photodynamic therapy (PDT) to Lucentis (ranibizumab) gained about 4 more letters at 2 years, closed more polyps and needed half the injections. The visual edge was modest, and the polyp-closure figures rest on a subset of eyes.

Study snapshot

Design
Phase IV, randomized 1:1, double-masked, sham-controlled, multicenter trial in Asia. Testing was stepwise: noninferiority of combination therapy (5-letter margin) and superior polyp regression first, then superiority on vision
Population
Asian adults with symptomatic macular PCV confirmed by a central reading center on indocyanine green angiography (ICGA). Mean age 68.1 years; mean baseline VA 61.1 letters
N
322 randomized: 168 Lucentis plus PDT, 154 Lucentis plus sham PDT
Both arms
Lucentis 0.5 mg monthly for 3 months, then as needed (PRN)
Combination arm
Standard-fluence verteporfin PDT on day 1, repeated PRN when active polyps were present. The other arm got sham PDT on the same schedule
Primary outcome
Change in best-corrected VA, with complete polyp regression on ICGA as the key anatomic outcome
Funding
Not verified. Several authors are Novartis employees

Read with

  • Same trialKoh et al., JAMA Ophthalmol 2017The 12-month primary report.
  • Earlier pilotKoh et al., Retina 2012 (EVEREST)61 patients, 6 months: PDT alone or with Lucentis closed more polyps than Lucentis alone (71.4% and 77.8% vs 28.6%). Too small to compare vision.
  • Same trialCheung et al., Retina 2021Post hoc: younger age and lower baseline VA predicted bigger gains, and polyp area may help choose between monotherapy and combination.

Effect

Combination therapy met noninferiority and then superiority for vision. At 2 years the gap was 4.1 letters (95% CI 1.0 to 7.2), under one ETDRS line on average, so the visual advantage is real but modest. Polyp closure showed the clearest difference.

The burden did not go away

Fewer injections is not treatment-free. The combination arm also needed a median of 2 PDT sessions over 2 years, and 44.6% needed only 1. At 12 months the only serious ocular adverse event was vitreous hemorrhage (1 patient with combination, 3 with Lucentis alone).

What this was not

It tested PDT added to Lucentis on a PRN schedule, not other anti-VEGF drugs or a fixed schedule, so the result should not be stretched to every regimen. Every participant was Asian, and eyes were selected by ICGA-confirmed PCV, which many clinics do not routinely have. It also does not say what PDT adds in eyes already responding well to anti-VEGF alone.

How far to trust it

Moderate for the visual gain, polyp closure and injection savings with this regimen

A reasonably large, double-masked trial with central grading, and the 12- and 24-month results agree. I hold it at moderate because the vision gap is small relative to its confidence interval, and the 24-month polyp regression was reported in only 143 and 86 eyes.

What I'd change

Report vision outcomes for all randomized eyes, with a clear plan for missing data. Add an arm with Eylea (aflibercept), since many patients now start on it, and a treat-and-extend schedule. Include non-Asian patients, and follow long enough to see whether closing polyps prevents later hemorrhage and recurrence.

Clinical significance takeaway

For ICGA-confirmed symptomatic PCV, discuss anti-VEGF with adjunctive verteporfin PDT. EVEREST II supports prompt PDT with Lucentis: modestly better vision, more polyp closure and fewer injections. PDT adds its own visits and cost, so the choice depends on polyp activity, access to PDT and patient preference. I would not assume the same benefit with other anti-VEGF drugs or schedules.

Bottom line

EVEREST II 2020: for symptomatic PCV, adding verteporfin PDT to Lucentis (ranibizumab) beat Lucentis alone at 2 years (+9.6 vs +5.5 letters) with half the injections; discuss combination treatment, otherwise anti-VEGF alone.
