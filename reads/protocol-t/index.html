Protocol T: aflibercept, bevacizumab or ranibizumab for diabetic macular edema

All three drugs improved vision. When starting vision was 20/50 or worse, aflibercept gave about 1.3 more chart lines than bevacizumab at one year, an edge that narrowed by two years.

Study snapshot

Design
Randomized comparative-effectiveness trial, DRCR Retina Network, 89 US sites
Population
Center-involved diabetic macular edema with vision 20/32 to 20/320; no anti-VEGF in the past 12 months
N
660 participants, one study eye each
Arms
Aflibercept 2.0 mg (224), bevacizumab 1.25 mg, repackaged (218), ranibizumab 0.3 mg (218)
Treatment
Visits every 4 weeks; injections followed a protocol algorithm. Focal/grid laser could be added from week 24 for persistent, nonimproving edema
Primary outcome
Mean change in visual acuity at 1 year
Follow-up
1 year (primary); 2-year results reported in 2016
Funding
National Institutes of Health; Regeneron and Genentech supplied aflibercept and ranibizumab
Registration
NCT01627249

Read with

  • 2-year follow-upWells et al., Ophthalmology 2016Whether aflibercept's advantage at 20/50 or worse lasted.
  • Next trialProtocol AC, Jhaveri et al., NEJM 2022Bevacizumab first with a switch to aflibercept, the way many clinics and insurers do it.

What they found

Protocol T, 1 year

Starting vision affected the treatment comparison. At 20/32–20/40, there was little difference in average vision gains. At 20/50 or worse, aflibercept produced greater gains than either comparator at one year.

Mean letters gained at 1 year, by starting vision
Starting visionAfliberceptBevacizumabRanibizumab
20/32–20/40 (51% of eyes)+8.0+7.5+8.3
20/50 or worse+18.9+11.8+14.2

For eyes starting at 20/50 or worse, aflibercept's adjusted advantage over bevacizumab was 6.5 letters at one year, about 1.3 chart lines (95% CI 2.9 to 10.1; P < .001), and over ranibizumab 4.7 letters (95% CI 1.4 to 8.0; P = .003). The adjustment explains why this differs from simply subtracting the raw group means. There were no significant differences in serious adverse events, deaths or major cardiovascular events.

Protocol T, 2 years · Ophthalmology 2016

At 20/50 or worse, mean gains were 18.1, 13.3 and 16.1 letters. Aflibercept still beat bevacizumab (P = .02), but its advantage over ranibizumab was no longer significant (P = .18). Injections fell in year 2 (median 5–6 in each group).

Protocol AC, 2 years · NEJM 2022

In eyes starting at 20/50 to 20/320, starting with bevacizumab and switching to aflibercept for a poor response matched aflibercept from the start: 14.0 vs 15.0 letters (adjusted difference 0.8, 95% CI −0.9 to 2.5). 70% of bevacizumab-first eyes switched.

How far to trust it

High for the 1-year result
Risk of bias, Protocol T (one-year visual acuity)
DomainConcernWhy
RandomizationLowCentral web allocation; permuted blocks; stratified by site and starting vision
Deviations from intended treatmentLowAlgorithm limited discretion; 99% of required injections delivered at completed visits. Investigators were unmasked
Missing dataLow96% completed, excluding deaths; multiple imputation and consistent sensitivity analyses
MeasurementLowStandardized vision testing; one-year testers and participants masked
Selective reportingLowRegistered (NCT01627249); the interaction with starting vision was prespecified, and the three comparisons were adjusted for multiple testing

These ratings apply to the primary vision outcome; they should not automatically be carried over to every safety or subgroup analysis.

Similar results are not proven equivalence. This was a superiority comparison; it did not establish that the drugs were interchangeable. "Aflibercept for worse vision" also needs a time point: at one year.

Group averages are not individual responses. Some eyes may improve substantially, others little, and others worsen.

Follow-up concerns need separating. Trial attrition was low, so missing outcomes are unlikely to distort the comparison. Real-world feasibility is a different question: can the patient attend frequent appointments and receive repeated treatment? That mainly affects applicability to a safety-net clinic like County, not bias in the trial.

The number that matters

6.5
Letters, aflibercept over bevacizumab

Adjusted advantage at one year in eyes starting at 20/50 or worse, about 1.3 chart lines (95% CI 2.9 to 10.1).

What I'd change

Test the strategy clinics actually use, bevacizumab first with a switch for poor response, instead of three fixed drugs. Protocol AC later did exactly this.

Clinical significance takeaway

For patients resembling the trial population, starting visual acuity helps guide drug selection: aflibercept had a meaningful one-year advantage when vision was 20/50 or worse, while milder impairment showed little average separation. With milder impairment, I'd weigh access and cost more heavily. For either patient, I'd assess whether repeated treatment and follow-up are feasible.

Bottom line

Starting vision should guide anti-VEGF choice for DME: aflibercept had a meaningful one-year edge at 20/50 or worse, but Protocol AC shows starting bevacizumab and switching for poor response reaches similar vision by two years.
