Landmark trials

Papers that changed guidelines or standard practice.

  1. Nov 2026

    Protocol T: aflibercept, bevacizumab or ranibizumab for diabetic macular edema

    NEJM, 2015 Retina RCT Read with the 2-year results and Protocol AC
    High certainty, 1-year result

    Starting vision should guide anti-VEGF choice for DME: aflibercept had a meaningful one-year edge at 20/50 or worse, but Protocol AC shows starting bevacizumab and switching for poor response reaches similar vision by two years.

  2. Nov 2026

    EVS: immediate vitrectomy vs tap and inject for endophthalmitis after cataract surgery

    Arch Ophthalmol, 1995 Retina RCT Read with the 2001 diabetes analysis
    Moderate certainty, light-perception result

    EVS still sets the on-call rule for post-cataract endophthalmitis: light-perception-only vision means immediate vitrectomy; better vision can start with tap and inject.

  3. Nov 2026

    LiGHT: laser vs eye drops as first treatment for glaucoma and ocular hypertension

    Lancet, 2019 Glaucoma RCT Read with the 6-year follow-up
    High certainty, 3-year result

    I would prefer SLT first for eligible patients who tolerate it, given the long-term personal medical and financial burden of daily drops.

  4. Nov 2026

    Protocol S: ranibizumab vs PRP for proliferative diabetic retinopathy

    JAMA, 2015 Retina RCT Read with CLARITY and the 5-year follow-up
    High certainty, 2-year result

    Protocol S supports anti-VEGF as an effective alternative, but its ongoing treatment burden makes follow-up reliability central to the decision.

Emerging research

Early-phase and new evidence that hasn't changed practice yet.

  1. Nov 2026

    PIONEER: optogenetic therapy for blindness from retinitis pigmentosa

    NEJM, 2026 Retina Early-phase trial Read with the 2021 case report
    Very low certainty, visual-function benefit

    Optogenetic therapy may give limited, goggle-dependent visual function to some blind RP patients, but it needs controlled trials with prespecified outcomes before it can be called effective.
