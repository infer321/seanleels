Landmark trials

Papers that changed guidelines or standard practice.

  1. Nov 2026

    SCORE2: Avastin vs Eylea for central retinal vein occlusion

    JAMA, May 2017RetinaRCT
    Highcertainty, 6-month vision noninferiority with monthly dosing

    SCORE2 2017: for macular edema from CRVO or hemiretinal RVO, monthly Avastin (bevacizumab) was noninferior to Eylea (aflibercept) for vision at 6 months (+18.6 vs +18.9 letters); start with Avastin where acceptable, otherwise Eylea.

  2. Nov 2026

    BRAVO: Lucentis for branch retinal vein occlusion

    Ophthalmology, Jun 2010RetinaRCT
    Highcertainty, 6-month benefit over sham

    BRAVO 2010: for macular edema from BRVO, monthly Lucentis (ranibizumab) 0.5 mg beat sham at 6 months (+18.3 vs +7.3 letters); start anti-VEGF with grid laser as rescue, otherwise reserve treatment for vision-reducing edema.

  3. Nov 2026

    PEDIG amblyopia trials: patching or atropine

    Arch Ophthalmol, 2002 · 2003 · 2004 Pediatrics RCT
    High certainty, patching and atropine both help; moderate for the exact regimen cut-offs

    PEDIG ATS-1 2002: for moderate amblyopia in children aged 3 to under 7, patching and atropine improved vision about 3 lines at 6 months (3.16 vs 2.84), and later trials showed 2 hours of patching or weekend atropine is enough; fix refractive error first, otherwise use 6 hours of patching for severe cases.

  4. Nov 2026

    PTVT and TVT: tube or trabeculectomy

    Am J Ophthalmol, 2012 · Ophthalmology, 2022 Glaucoma RCT
    Moderate certainty, TVT failure result; lower for PTVT, which is inconclusive on failure

    PTVT 2022 and TVT 2012: with no prior incisional surgery, tube and trabeculectomy performed similarly (failure 42% vs 35%), so choose by drop burden and follow-up; after prior cataract or glaucoma surgery, the Baerveldt tube failed less than trabeculectomy (29.8% vs 46.9%).

  5. Nov 2026

    TAGS: early trabeculectomy for advanced glaucoma

    BMJ, May 2021 Glaucoma RCT
    Moderate certainty, no quality-of-life difference at 2 years; high for the pressure difference

    TAGS 2021: for newly diagnosed advanced open-angle glaucoma, primary MMC trabeculectomy lowered pressure more than drops (12.4 vs 15.1 mmHg) without changing quality of life at 2 years; discuss early surgery, otherwise escalate drops and operate if pressure stays uncontrolled.

  6. Nov 2026

    CRUISE: Lucentis for central retinal vein occlusion

    Ophthalmology, Apr 2010 Retina RCT
    High certainty, 6-month benefit over sham; moderate for the cost of delay

    CRUISE 2010: for macular edema from CRVO with VA 20/40 to 20/320, monthly Lucentis (ranibizumab) 0.5 mg beat sham at 6 months (+14.9 vs +0.8 letters); start promptly and keep monthly review, since a 6-month delay never fully caught up.

  7. Nov 2026

    EAGLE: early lens extraction for angle closure

    Lancet, Oct 2016 Glaucoma RCT
    Moderate certainty, less treatment burden; low for blindness prevention

    EAGLE 2016: for a patient 50 or older with newly diagnosed PAC and IOP of 30 mm Hg or higher, or PACG, clear-lens extraction needed fewer drops than laser iridotomy plus drops (0.4 vs 1.3 at 3 years); discuss early lens extraction with glaucoma, otherwise iridotomy with drops.

  8. Nov 2026

    EAGLE: intra-arterial thrombolysis for CRAO

    Ophthalmology, Jul 2010 Retina RCT
    Low certainty, small trial stopped early with late treatment

    EAGLE 2010: for acute nonarteritic CRAO within 20 hours, intra-arterial rtPA was no better than conservative care for vision (57.1% vs 60.0% improved ≥0.3 logMAR) and had more adverse reactions (37.1% vs 4.3%); don't offer it routinely, and send for immediate stroke-center evaluation instead.

  9. Nov 2026

    ESCRS: intracameral cefuroxime after cataract surgery

    J Cataract Refract Surg, Jun 2007 Cataract RCT
    Moderate certainty, substantial benefit; exact size imprecise

    ESCRS 2007: for cataract surgery, intracameral cefuroxime 1 mg at the end of the case cut endophthalmitis about fivefold (0.06% vs 0.30%, NNT about 430); give an intracameral antibiotic with povidone-iodine antisepsis, checking allergy history, and keep postoperative drops unless a trial shows they are unnecessary.

  10. Nov 2026

    Protocol V: observation for DME with good vision

    JAMA, May 2019 Retina RCT Read with Protocol T
    Moderate certainty, observation with rescue is reasonable, not shown equivalent

    Protocol V 2019: for center-involved DME with VA 20/25 or better, initial observation with Eylea (aflibercept) rescue had similar 2-year vision loss to immediate Eylea (19% vs 16% lost ≥5 letters, not significant); observe if follow-up is reliable, otherwise treat.

  11. Nov 2026

    AREDS2: lutein, zeaxanthin and omega-3 for AMD

    JAMA, May 2013 Retina RCT Read with the original AREDS trial
    High certainty, no added benefit from omega-3 or extra lutein/zeaxanthin

    AREDS2 2013: for intermediate AMD, adding omega-3 or extra lutein/zeaxanthin to AREDS doesn't further cut progression (HR 0.90, not significant); use a beta-carotene-free formula with lutein/zeaxanthin instead, especially with any smoking history.

  12. Nov 2026

    ONTT: corticosteroids for acute optic neuritis

    NEJM, Feb 1992 Neuro-ophthalmology RCT Read with the 1-year follow-up and a 2018 oral-dose trial
    Moderate certainty, speed of recovery

    ONTT 1992: for acute optic neuritis, IV methylprednisolone speeds recovery but not 6-month acuity; offer it if faster recovery matters (high-dose oral is a reasonable alternative), but avoid oral prednisone 1 mg/kg alone (recurrence RR 1.79).

  13. Nov 2026

    CATT and IVAN: Avastin vs Lucentis for neovascular AMD

    NEJM, 2011 · Lancet, 2013 Retina RCT Read with IVAN's 1-year results
    High certainty, vision on the same schedule

    CATT 2011: for wet AMD, Avastin matches Lucentis for 1-year vision at 1/40 the cost; first line, with monthly visits even if dosing PRN.

  14. Nov 2026

    OHTS: treating ocular hypertension to prevent glaucoma

    Arch Ophthalmol, 2002 Glaucoma RCT Read with the risk-factor analysis and 20-year follow-up
    High certainty, 5-year result

    OHTS 2002: for OHT, IOP-lowering drops halve 5-year conversion to glaucoma (9.5% → 4.4%); treat moderate to high risk (older age, high IOP, thin cornea, large C/D), otherwise observe.

  15. Nov 2026

    Protocol T: Eylea, Avastin or Lucentis for diabetic macular edema

    NEJM, 2015 Retina RCT Read with the 2-year results and Protocol AC
    High certainty, 1-year result

    Protocol T 2015: for DME with VA 20/50 or worse, Eylea beats Avastin at 1 year (+6.5 letters); if step therapy is required, start Avastin and switch (Protocol AC).

  16. Nov 2026

    EVS: immediate vitrectomy vs tap and inject for endophthalmitis after cataract surgery

    Arch Ophthalmol, 1995 Retina RCT Read with the 2001 diabetes analysis
    Moderate certainty, light-perception result

    EVS 1995: for post-cataract endophthalmitis, LP-only vision → immediate PPV + intravitreal antibiotics (33% vs 11% reach 20/40); HM or better → tap and inject; no IV antibiotics.

  17. Nov 2026

    LiGHT: laser vs eye drops as first treatment for glaucoma and ocular hypertension

    Lancet, 2019 Glaucoma RCT Read with the 6-year follow-up
    High certainty, 3-year result

    LiGHT 2019: for new OAG or OHT, SLT first matches drops on quality of life and keeps 74% drop-free at 3 years; offer first if tolerated.

  18. Nov 2026

    Protocol S: Lucentis vs PRP for proliferative diabetic retinopathy

    JAMA, 2015 Retina RCT Read with CLARITY and the 5-year follow-up
    High certainty, 2-year result

    Protocol S 2015: for PDR, Lucentis is noninferior to PRP at 2 years with less DME (9% vs 28%); anti-VEGF if DME, PRP if follow-up is unreliable.

Emerging research

Early-phase trials and evidence that hasn't changed guidelines yet.

  1. Nov 2026

    EVEREST II: PDT plus Lucentis for polypoidal choroidal vasculopathy

    JAMA Ophthalmol, 2017 · 2020 Retina RCT
    Moderate certainty, visual gain, polyp closure and injection savings with this regimen

    EVEREST II 2020: for symptomatic PCV, adding verteporfin PDT to Lucentis (ranibizumab) beat Lucentis alone at 2 years (+9.6 vs +5.5 letters) with half the injections; discuss combination treatment, otherwise anti-VEGF alone.

  2. Nov 2026

    LAMP and CHAMP: low-dose atropine for childhood myopia

    Ophthalmology, 2019 · JAMA Ophthalmol, 2023 Pediatrics RCT Read with the LAMP 2-year follow-up
    Moderate certainty, 0.05% slows progression over 1 to 2 years

    LAMP 2019: for progressing childhood myopia, atropine 0.05% nightly slowed 1-year progression most (−0.27 vs −0.81 D on placebo); start there and recheck refraction and axial length every 4 months, otherwise 0.01% (CHAMP 2023, supportive only: primary endpoint failed).

  3. Nov 2026

    Trabeculotomy vs combined surgery for congenital glaucoma

    Ophthalmology, Nov 2026 Glaucoma Pediatrics RCT
    Moderate certainty, a reasonable alternative, not shown equivalent

    Trabeculotomy vs combined surgery 2026: for surgery-naive primary congenital glaucoma with corneal diameter 12–14 mm, external trabeculotomy was noninferior to combined trabeculotomy-trabeculectomy with mitomycin-C at 3 years (82.3% vs 84.7%, CI lower bound −11.7 vs a −15 margin); offer it first and individualize the target IOP, otherwise combined surgery if the disease is more severe.

  4. Nov 2026

    PIONEER: optogenetic therapy for blindness from retinitis pigmentosa

    NEJM, 2026 Retina Early-phase trial Read with the 2021 case report
    Very low certainty, visual-function benefit

    PIONEER 2026: for end-stage RP, GS030 optogenetics looked safe in 10 patients, but efficacy is unproven; refer to trials and low-vision rehab.
