Landmark trials

Papers that changed guidelines or standard practice.

  1. Nov 2026

    LAMP and CHAMP: low-dose atropine for childhood myopia

    Ophthalmology, 2019 · JAMA Ophthalmol, 2023 Pediatrics RCT Read with the LAMP 2-year follow-up
    Moderate certainty, 0.05% slows progression over 1 to 2 years

    LAMP 2019: for progressing childhood myopia, atropine 0.05% nightly slowed 1-year progression most (−0.27 vs −0.81 D on placebo); start there and recheck refraction and axial length every 4 months, otherwise 0.01% (CHAMP 2023, supportive only: primary endpoint failed).

  2. Nov 2026

    Protocol V: observation for DME with good vision

    JAMA, May 2019 Retina RCT Read with Protocol T
    Moderate certainty, observation with rescue is reasonable, not shown equivalent

    Protocol V 2019: for center-involved DME with VA 20/25 or better, initial observation with Eylea (aflibercept) rescue had similar 2-year vision loss to immediate Eylea (19% vs 16% lost ≥5 letters, not significant); observe if follow-up is reliable, otherwise treat.

  3. Nov 2026

    AREDS2: lutein, zeaxanthin and omega-3 for AMD

    JAMA, May 2013 Retina RCT Read with the original AREDS trial
    High certainty, no added benefit from omega-3 or extra lutein/zeaxanthin

    AREDS2 2013: for intermediate AMD, adding omega-3 or extra lutein/zeaxanthin to AREDS doesn't further cut progression (HR 0.90, not significant); use a beta-carotene-free formula with lutein/zeaxanthin instead, especially with any smoking history.

  4. Nov 2026

    ONTT: corticosteroids for acute optic neuritis

    NEJM, Feb 1992 Neuro-ophthalmology RCT Read with the 1-year follow-up and a 2018 oral-dose trial
    Moderate certainty, speed of recovery

    ONTT 1992: for acute optic neuritis, IV methylprednisolone speeds recovery but not 6-month acuity; offer it if faster recovery matters (high-dose oral is a reasonable alternative), but avoid oral prednisone 1 mg/kg alone (recurrence RR 1.79).

  5. Nov 2026

    CATT and IVAN: Avastin vs Lucentis for neovascular AMD

    NEJM, 2011 · Lancet, 2013 Retina RCT Read with IVAN's 1-year results
    High certainty, vision on the same schedule

    CATT 2011: for wet AMD, Avastin matches Lucentis for 1-year vision at 1/40 the cost; first line, with monthly visits even if dosing PRN.

  6. Nov 2026

    OHTS: treating ocular hypertension to prevent glaucoma

    Arch Ophthalmol, 2002 Glaucoma RCT Read with the risk-factor analysis and 20-year follow-up
    High certainty, 5-year result

    OHTS 2002: for OHT, IOP-lowering drops halve 5-year conversion to glaucoma (9.5% → 4.4%); treat moderate to high risk (older age, high IOP, thin cornea, large C/D), otherwise observe.

  7. Nov 2026

    Protocol T: Eylea, Avastin or Lucentis for diabetic macular edema

    NEJM, 2015 Retina RCT Read with the 2-year results and Protocol AC
    High certainty, 1-year result

    Protocol T 2015: for DME with VA 20/50 or worse, Eylea beats Avastin at 1 year (+6.5 letters); if step therapy is required, start Avastin and switch (Protocol AC).

  8. Nov 2026

    EVS: immediate vitrectomy vs tap and inject for endophthalmitis after cataract surgery

    Arch Ophthalmol, 1995 Retina RCT Read with the 2001 diabetes analysis
    Moderate certainty, light-perception result

    EVS 1995: for post-cataract endophthalmitis, LP-only vision → immediate PPV + intravitreal antibiotics (33% vs 11% reach 20/40); HM or better → tap and inject; no IV antibiotics.

  9. Nov 2026

    LiGHT: laser vs eye drops as first treatment for glaucoma and ocular hypertension

    Lancet, 2019 Glaucoma RCT Read with the 6-year follow-up
    High certainty, 3-year result

    LiGHT 2019: for new OAG or OHT, SLT first matches drops on quality of life and keeps 74% drop-free at 3 years; offer first if tolerated.

  10. Nov 2026

    Protocol S: Lucentis vs PRP for proliferative diabetic retinopathy

    JAMA, 2015 Retina RCT Read with CLARITY and the 5-year follow-up
    High certainty, 2-year result

    Protocol S 2015: for PDR, Lucentis is noninferior to PRP at 2 years with less DME (9% vs 28%); anti-VEGF if DME, PRP if follow-up is unreliable.

Emerging research

Early-phase and new evidence that hasn't changed practice yet.

  1. Nov 2026

    PIONEER: optogenetic therapy for blindness from retinitis pigmentosa

    NEJM, 2026 Retina Early-phase trial Read with the 2021 case report
    Very low certainty, visual-function benefit

    PIONEER 2026: for end-stage RP, GS030 optogenetics looked safe in 10 patients, but efficacy is unproven; refer to trials and low-vision rehab.
