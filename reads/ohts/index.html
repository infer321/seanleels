OHTS: treating ocular hypertension to prevent glaucoma

Drops halved the 5-year risk of converting to glaucoma, but over 20 years only a quarter of participants lost visual field: a case for treating by risk, not by pressure alone.

Study snapshot

Design
Randomized trial of pressure-lowering drops versus close observation, 22 US clinics; intention-to-treat analysis
Population
Ocular hypertension without established glaucomatous damage; age 40–80; IOP 24–32 mm Hg in one eye and 21–32 in the other
N
1,636 participants
Intervention
Commercially available topical drops; target IOP reduction of 20% or more, plus IOP 24 mm Hg or less
Comparator
Close observation
Primary outcome
Reproducible glaucomatous disc deterioration or visual-field abnormality, judged by masked readers and endpoint committee; not blindness
Follow-up
Randomized phase to 2002 (5-year result); 20-year follow-up reported in 2021
Funding
National Eye Institute and other sources
Registration
NCT00000125

What they found

OHTS, 5 years

Glaucoma developed in 4.4% with medication versus 9.5% with observation (hazard ratio 0.40, 95% CI 0.27 to 0.59). Pressure fell 22.5% on average with medication and 4.0% with observation.

Treatment reduced conversion risk. This does not establish that every patient with elevated pressure needs treatment.

These are predictors; identifying a predictor does not prove that changing it changes the outcome.

OHTS, 20 years · JAMA Ophthalmol 2021

Estimated cumulative glaucoma incidence was 45.6% (49.3% in the original observation group, 41.9% in the medication group), and estimated incidence of glaucomatous visual-field loss was 25.2%. By baseline risk, incidence was 31.7%, 47.6% and 59.8% in the low, medium and high tertiles. These are survival estimates, not simple percentages of everyone originally enrolled.

Both groups were offered medication from 2002, and treatment became clinician-directed after 2008. Therefore, this was not 20 years of drops versus 20 years untreated. The follow-up describes long-term prognosis under changing treatment; it cannot cleanly estimate the effect of continuous treatment versus continuous observation.

The number that matters

~20
Number needed to treat for 5 years

Absolute reduction of 5.1 percentage points (9.5% vs 4.4%): approximately 20 people treated for five years to prevent or delay one glaucoma endpoint. This is calculated from the reported five-year risks.

What I'd change

Make visual-field loss or visual function the primary outcome, since "conversion" includes disc changes that may never affect what the patient sees.

Clinical significance takeaway

OHTS supports basing preventive treatment on conversion risk, life expectancy, treatment burden, and ability to monitor, rather than pressure alone.

For a patient with ocular hypertension, I'd first assess their glaucoma risk. If treatment is warranted and they prefer drops, I'd start latanoprost 0.005%, one drop in the affected eye each evening, aim for an initial 20% IOP reduction, and bring them back in 4–6 weeks to check pressure, adherence, and side effects.

Bottom line

Per OHTS (2002), for ocular hypertension, topical drops lowering IOP by at least 20% halved 5-year conversion to glaucoma (4.4% vs 9.5%; NNT about 20), so I'd start latanoprost 0.005% nightly for this patient if their baseline risk is moderate or high (older age, higher IOP, thinner cornea, larger cup-to-disc ratio), and observe if it's low.
