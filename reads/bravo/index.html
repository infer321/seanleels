★ Landmark RetinaRCTOphthalmology, Jun 2010

BRAVO: Lucentis for branch retinal vein occlusion

Monthly Lucentis (ranibizumab) gained about 17 to 18 letters at 6 months in macular edema from branch retinal vein occlusion (BRVO), against 7 letters with sham. Sham eyes were not untreated: more than half got rescue laser.

Study snapshot

Design
Phase III, randomized 1:1:1, sham-controlled, double-masked, multicenter trial
Population
397 patients with macular edema following BRVO
Treatment
Monthly intraocular Lucentis 0.3 mg, Lucentis 0.5 mg, or sham injections for 6 months. Rescue grid laser was allowed
Primary outcome
Mean change in best-corrected VA letter score at month 6
Funding
Not verified

Effect

Both doses beat sham for every vision outcome (P < .0001 for each). For 0.5 mg the mean gain was 18.3 letters (95% CI 16.0 to 20.6) against 7.3 for sham (95% CI 5.1 to 9.5). These are visual benefits, not only thinner OCT.

Sham was not untreated

Over half of sham eyes (54.5%) received rescue grid laser, against about a fifth of treated eyes. The sham arm therefore reflects injection-free care plus laser as needed, and its 7-letter gain is partly the natural course and partly laser. The treatment effect is still large, but it is the effect of Lucentis against that comparator.

What this was not

It tested benefit against sham, not one anti-VEGF drug against another or against laser given first. It followed patients for 6 months in the sham-controlled phase, so it does not show how many injections are needed long term, and the abstract does not address severely ischemic eyes.

How far to trust it

Highfor the 6-month benefit over sham

A large, double-masked, sham-controlled trial with a big, consistent effect on vision and thickness. The sham comparator was not untreated, which if anything narrows the apparent gap, and the sham-controlled phase was only 6 months.

What I'd change

Compare monthly injections, as-needed injections and laser first on the same schedule. Include a head-to-head against off-label Avastin (bevacizumab), report ischemia on widefield angiography, and report outcomes after the sham arm crossed over so the cost of delay can be seen.

Clinical significance takeaway

For vision-threatening macular edema from BRVO, start anti-VEGF, reassess VA and OCT monthly, and keep grid laser as a rescue if edema persists. Do not treat every BRVO automatically: treat when the edema is reducing vision. Assess ischemia and keep watching for neovascularization even when OCT improves, and address blood pressure and vascular risk.

Bottom line

BRAVO 2010: for macular edema from BRVO, monthly Lucentis (ranibizumab) 0.5 mg beat sham at 6 months (+18.3 vs +7.3 letters); start anti-VEGF with grid laser as rescue, otherwise reserve treatment for vision-reducing edema.
