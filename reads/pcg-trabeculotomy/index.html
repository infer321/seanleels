Trabeculotomy vs combined surgery for congenital glaucoma

Trabeculotomy met the trial's noninferiority criterion against combined trabeculotomy-trabeculectomy at 3 years. The margin was wide, so the result doesn't show the two operations are clinically equal.

Study snapshot

Design
Prospective, randomized, single-center noninferiority trial at Zhongshan Ophthalmic Center, Guangzhou, China
Population
Children aged 0 to 3 years with primary congenital glaucoma, horizontal corneal diameter 12 to 14 mm and no previous intraocular or laser surgery
N
248 children, 124 per group, one eye each
Interventions
Conventional external trabeculotomy with 120° dissection of Schlemm's canal, or combined trabeculotomy-trabeculectomy with mitomycin-C 0.3 mg/mL for 3 minutes
Primary outcome
Qualified surgical success at 36 months, noninferiority margin −15 percentage points
Follow-up
3 years
Registration
ChiCTR-IOR-14005588
Funding
Not verified

Read with

  • ProtocolFang et al., BMJ Open 2020Published design, techniques, definitions of success, randomization method and the authors' own caveat about the IOP threshold.

What they found

What counted as success

Qualified success required all three of the following: an IOP of 5 to 21 mmHg, with or without glaucoma medication; no vision-threatening complication; and no additional glaucoma surgery. Success here doesn't mean medication-free control, and it doesn't by itself mean preserved vision.

Results at 3 years
OutcomeTrabeculotomyCombined surgery
Qualified success, intention to treat82.3% (102 of 124)84.7% (105 of 124)
Qualified success, per protocol90.3%92.9%
Mean IOP reduction12.1 mmHg13.2 mmHg
Any adverse event45.2%54.0%

The intention-to-treat difference was −2.4 percentage points (95% CI −11.7 to +6.9; P = 0.004 for noninferiority). The per-protocol difference was −2.6 points (95% CI −10.3 to 4.9). The IOP reduction did not differ significantly (P = 0.067), nor did adverse events (P = 0.162). Hyphema was the most common adverse event in both groups and usually resolved on its own.

How to read the noninferiority result

  • It passed because the lower bound of the confidence interval, −11.7, stayed above the −15 margin.
  • The interval still allows trabeculotomy to have about 12 fewer successes per 100 children.
  • A −15 point margin would accept 70% success against 85%, which would matter to a family.
  • The P value tests noninferiority. It doesn't show superiority, and fewer adverse events in the trabeculotomy group doesn't prove greater safety.

Details that limit generalizing

The protocol specified conventional external trabeculotomy, so the result doesn't carry over to 360° techniques. The combined operation used mitomycin-C. Children with severe corneal clouding or secondary congenital glaucoma were excluded, as were those who couldn't attend follow-up. The authors themselves noted that an IOP threshold of 21 mmHg or below may be too permissive in very young children.

The unresolved question

Success rises from about 83% intention to treat to about 91% per protocol. The protocol's withdrawal criteria, which include being unable to dissect Schlemm's canal for 120°, may explain part of this, but I couldn't confirm that without the CONSORT flow, exclusions and missing-data rules. Similar differences between the arms in both analyses support the noninferiority result, but they don't explain the large change in absolute success.

How far to trust it

Moderate that trabeculotomy is a reasonable alternative, not that it is equivalent

A randomized trial of 248 children with 3-year follow-up, which its protocol calls the largest clinical trial in pediatric glaucoma. The rating stops at moderate because the margin was wide, the trial was in a single center, and the intention-to-treat and per-protocol success rates differ by about 8 points.

I could read the abstract and the published protocol, not the full 2026 report, so these ratings are provisional and are not a completed formal RoB 2 assessment. The wide margin affects how the result is interpreted, and the single-center setting limits generalizability. Neither automatically means a high risk of bias.

What I'd change

Use a narrower margin, report a CONSORT flow with the reasons for every exclusion and withdrawal, define success with a lower IOP ceiling for infants, and add multicenter sites outside one country, so the result can be applied beyond this population.

Clinical significance takeaway

Trabeculotomy is a supported alternative for this selected population, but the trial doesn't establish equal efficacy or superior safety. For surgery-naive congenital glaucoma with a corneal diameter of 12 to 14 mm, I'd discuss trabeculotomy as an initial option with pediatric glaucoma. I'd individualize the target pressure and consider disease severity, because the margin allowed a substantial loss of success.

Bottom line

Trabeculotomy vs combined surgery 2026: for surgery-naive primary congenital glaucoma with corneal diameter 12–14 mm, external trabeculotomy was noninferior to combined trabeculotomy-trabeculectomy with mitomycin-C at 3 years (82.3% vs 84.7%, CI lower bound −11.7 vs a −15 margin); offer it first and individualize the target IOP, otherwise combined surgery if the disease is more severe.
