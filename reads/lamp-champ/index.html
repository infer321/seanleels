LAMP and CHAMP: low-dose atropine for childhood myopia

In LAMP, atropine 0.05% slowed myopia progression most over one year. CHAMP's primary endpoint for 0.02% failed, so its favorable 0.01% results are supportive rather than confirmatory.

Study snapshot

Design
Both: randomized, double-masked, placebo-controlled. LAMP: four arms, 1 year. CHAMP: three arms, 3 years, phase 3
Population
LAMP: children aged 4 to 12 with myopia of at least −1.0 D and astigmatism of −2.5 D or less. CHAMP: children aged 3 to 16 with myopia of −0.50 to −6.00 D and astigmatism of −1.50 D or less, at sites in North America and Europe
N
LAMP: 438. CHAMP: 576 randomized; 489 aged 6 to 10 formed the primary analysis set
Interventions
LAMP: atropine 0.05%, 0.025% or 0.01% nightly in both eyes. CHAMP: preservative-free atropine 0.01% or 0.02% nightly in both eyes
Comparator
Placebo drops
Primary outcome
LAMP: change in cycloplegic spherical equivalent; axial elongation secondary. CHAMP: proportion of eyes progressing less than 0.50 D at 3 years, 0.02% vs placebo
Funding
CHAMP: sponsored by Vyluma, the maker of the formulation. LAMP: not verified
Registration
CHAMP: NCT03350620

Read with

  • 2-year follow-upLAMP phase 2, Yam et al., Ophthalmology 2020Over 2 years, 0.05% remained the most effective concentration, with about double the effect of 0.01%.
  • US trial of 0.01%Repka et al., JAMA Ophthalmol 2023In 187 US children, 0.01% did not slow progression or axial elongation compared with placebo.
  • After stoppingCHAMP year 4, Zadnik et al., Ophthalmic Physiol Opt 2026Little rebound in progression in the year after stopping 0.01%.

The primary result was negative

The prespecified primary comparison, responders on 0.02% vs placebo, was not significant (odds ratio 1.77, 95% CI 0.50 to 6.26, P = 0.37). The trial tested its endpoints in a fixed sequence, and the paper itself says the later secondary endpoints should not be considered formally significant. That includes the favorable 0.01% results, even though their P values were below .05 (0.24 D less progression, P < 0.001; 0.13 mm less elongation, P < 0.001).

So "CHAMP proved 0.01% works" overstates what the trial can confirm. The results for 0.01% are consistent across responders, refraction and axial length, which makes them supportive. A post hoc comparison of 0.01% and 0.02% did not show a difference (OR 2.6, 95% CI 0.77 to 8.57), so CHAMP does not show 0.01% is better than 0.02%.

Safety and follow-up

CHAMP had no serious ocular adverse events. Of 576 randomized children, 17.2% (99) discontinued, and the models assumed data were missing at random with no imputation. In LAMP, accommodation fell by 1.98 D at 0.05% and 1.61 D at 0.025%, against about 0.3 D with 0.01% or placebo.

What neither trial shows

Neither report settles when to stop treatment, and neither shows that slowing progression prevents later retinal detachment or myopic maculopathy. The trials measured refraction and axial length.

How far to trust it

Moderate for 0.05% slowing progression over 1 to 2 years

LAMP is a masked, placebo-controlled trial with a clear dose-response, and a 2-year extension agreed with it. The rating stops at moderate because LAMP is a single report of 438 children with limited detail on dropout, CHAMP's primary endpoint failed, and a US trial of 0.01% found no effect. Neither trial measured the outcomes that matter later in life.

Less myopia progression than placebo. The LAMP difference is −0.27 D vs −0.81 D. The CHAMP result is a secondary endpoint, not formally significant under the trial's testing order.

What I'd change

Compare 0.05%, 0.025% and 0.01% against each other and against placebo in one population for at least 3 years, with a prespecified plan for multiple comparisons, axial length as a co-primary outcome, and follow-up after treatment stops.

Clinical significance takeaway

LAMP provides stronger direct support for 0.05% among its tested doses. CHAMP suggests a modest benefit from its 0.01% formulation but does not overturn LAMP or establish the best concentration across populations. For a child with progressing myopia, I'd discuss atropine 0.05%, one drop in each eye nightly, balancing slower progression against light sensitivity and near blur. I'd monitor cycloplegic refraction and axial length about every four months.

Bottom line

LAMP 2019: for progressing childhood myopia, atropine 0.05% nightly slowed 1-year progression most (−0.27 vs −0.81 D on placebo); start there and recheck refraction and axial length every 4 months, otherwise 0.01% (CHAMP 2023, supportive only: primary endpoint failed).
