PTVT and TVT: tube or trabeculectomy

Two sister trials asked which glaucoma operation to do. In eyes with no prior incisional surgery (PTVT), the Baerveldt tube and trabeculectomy performed similarly at 5 years, with fewer drops after trabeculectomy. In eyes with prior cataract surgery or trabeculectomy (TVT), the tube failed less often.

Study snapshot

Design
Two multicenter randomized trials by the same group, with the same Baerveldt 350 mm² tube and the same failure definition. The populations differ by surgical history, so they cannot be pooled or compared as if surgical history were randomized
TVT population
212 eyes, age 18 to 85, uncontrolled glaucoma (IOP 18 to 40 mmHg on maximum tolerated medications) with previous trabeculectomy and/or cataract extraction with IOL. 107 tube, 105 trabeculectomy. 17 centers
PTVT population
242 eyes with medically uncontrolled glaucoma and no previous incisional ocular surgery. 125 tube, 117 trabeculectomy. 16 centers
Trabeculectomy
With mitomycin C (MMC) 0.4 mg/mL: 4 minutes in TVT, 2 minutes in PTVT
Failure
IOP above 21 mmHg or not reduced by 20%, IOP 5 mmHg or less, reoperation for glaucoma, or loss of light perception
Funding
NIH-supported (research support listed in PubMed); sponsor details not verified

Effect

The surgical history decides which trial applies. With no prior incisional surgery, failure did not differ (42% vs 35%) and pressures were nearly the same, but trabeculectomy needed about one fewer drop. After prior cataract surgery or trabeculectomy, trabeculectomy failed more often (46.9% vs 29.8%) and was reoperated on three times as often (29% vs 9%). Failure figures are cumulative probabilities over 5 years, not simple proportions of eyes.

What this was not

"Primary" in PTVT means the first incisional operation, not untreated glaucoma, and prior cataract surgery alone puts an eye in the TVT population. PTVT did not prove the operations equivalent: its hazard ratio of 1.31 has a confidence interval from 0.86 to 2.01, so a real difference in either direction is still possible. Both trials used the Baerveldt 350, so the results should not be applied automatically to the Ahmed valve or other devices. Meeting the failure definition also leaves pressure that may be too high for an individual patient.

Safety

Early complications were more frequent after trabeculectomy in both trials, and late complications did not differ. That does not make tubes universally safer: complications that matter with tubes, such as exposure, diplopia and corneal endothelial damage, are part of the choice and were not tested as the primary comparison here.

How far to trust it

Moderate for TVT's failure result; lower for PTVT, which is inconclusive on failure

Both are well-run multicenter randomized trials with objective pressure outcomes. Surgeons and patients knew the allocation, and reoperation, one component of failure, was at the surgeon's discretion. PTVT is a null result with a wide interval. Both are 5-year trials, so longer-term durability is not shown.

What I'd change

Run one trial that randomizes within strata of surgical history, so the two populations can be compared directly. Test Ahmed and newer devices, and minimally invasive options, against trabeculectomy. Report patient-reported outcomes and the burden of post-operative visits, and follow past 5 years.

Clinical significance takeaway

First establish prior cataract and glaucoma surgery. With no prior incisional surgery, PTVT supports either operation, with fewer drops after trabeculectomy. After cataract surgery or a trabeculectomy, TVT supports a Baerveldt for lower failure and reoperation. Individualize by target pressure, conjunctival health, corneal health and ability to attend post-operative follow-up.

Bottom line

PTVT 2022 and TVT 2012: with no prior incisional surgery, tube and trabeculectomy performed similarly (failure 42% vs 35%), so choose by drop burden and follow-up; after prior cataract or glaucoma surgery, the Baerveldt tube failed less than trabeculectomy (29.8% vs 46.9%).
