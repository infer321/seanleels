Protocol V: observation for diabetic macular edema with good vision

For center-involved DME with vision of 20/25 or better, starting Eylea (aflibercept) right away did not significantly reduce 2-year vision loss compared with laser or with observation plus Eylea only if vision worsened.

Study snapshot

Design
Multicenter, randomized, three arms; patients and clinicians not masked to treatment
Population
Adults with type 1 or 2 diabetes and one study eye with center-involved DME and visual acuity of 20/25 or better, at 91 US and Canadian sites
N
702 participants (mean age 59; 38% female)
Interventions
Eylea (aflibercept) 2.0 mg as often as every 4 weeks (n = 226); focal/grid laser (n = 240); observation (n = 236)
Rescue
Laser and observation eyes received Eylea for a loss of at least 10 letters at any visit, or 5 to 9 letters at 2 consecutive visits
Primary outcome
Loss of at least 5 letters of visual acuity from baseline at 2 years
Follow-up
2 years; 625 of 681 (92%, excluding deaths) completed the 2-year visit
Funding
National Institutes of Health (DRCR Retina Network)

Read with

  • Observation armGlassman et al., JAMA Ophthalmol 2020Follow-up schedule and who needed rescue: 34% overall, more often with thicker macula, worse retinopathy or a treated fellow eye.
  • Low-contrast visionBeaulieu et al., Transl Vis Sci Technol 2021Low-contrast acuity change did not differ significantly, but the study may have been underpowered.

What they found

Vision at 2 years

A loss of at least 5 letters occurred in 16% (33/205) with Eylea, 17% (36/212) with laser and 19% (39/208) with observation. None of the differences was significant. Eylea vs observation: risk difference −3% (95% CI −11% to 4%), relative risk 0.83 (95% CI 0.55 to 1.27). Eylea vs laser: −2% (95% CI −9% to 5%).

How many needed rescue

Among eyes with vision loss, Eylea was started in 34% (80/236) of the observation group and 25% (60/240) of the laser group, so about two-thirds of observed eyes avoided injections over 2 years. In a later analysis of the observation arm, rescue was more likely with central subfield thickness of 300 μm or more (45% vs 26%), retinopathy severity level 47 or worse (51% vs 27%) or a fellow eye treated for DME in the first 4 months (52% vs 25%).

Safety

Antiplatelet Trialists' Collaboration vascular events (myocardial infarction, stroke, vascular or unknown death) occurred in 7% with Eylea, 5% with laser and 3% with observation. The trial was not designed to show whether that difference is caused by treatment.

What the result does and doesn't say

This tested observation with rescue as a strategy. "No significant difference" is not a demonstration that the strategies are equivalent or noninferior, and the confidence interval for Eylea vs observation (−11% to +4%) is wide enough to include a modest benefit from early treatment. The result applies to eyes with good starting vision and to patients who attend follow-up reliably.

How far to trust it

Moderate for observation with rescue being a reasonable strategy, not for equivalence

A large, well-run randomized trial with protocol-defined rescue and high 2-year follow-up. The rating stops at moderate because the confidence interval is wide, the trial was not designed to show equivalence, and the result depends on reliable follow-up.

The number that matters

−3%
Risk difference in 5-letter vision loss

Eylea vs observation at 2 years (95% CI −11% to +4%), not significant.

What I'd change

Prespecify a noninferiority margin for observation vs Eylea and power the trial for it, and mask visual acuity assessors at every visit, so "observation is reasonable" becomes a tested claim rather than the absence of a significant difference.

Clinical significance takeaway

With center-involved DME and 20/20 vision, I'd initially observe if follow-up is reliable. I'd repeat visual acuity and OCT in 8 weeks, then at 16 weeks, extending only if stable. I'd start Eylea 2 mg for a 10-letter decline once or a confirmed 5 to 9-letter decline. OCT worsening alone would prompt closer review.

Bottom line

Protocol V 2019: for center-involved DME with VA 20/25 or better, initial observation with Eylea (aflibercept) rescue had similar 2-year vision loss to immediate Eylea (19% vs 16% lost ≥5 letters, not significant); observe if follow-up is reliable, otherwise treat.
