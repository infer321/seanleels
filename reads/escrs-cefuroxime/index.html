ESCRS: intracameral cefuroxime after cataract surgery

A single dose of cefuroxime 1 mg in the anterior chamber at the end of cataract surgery cut endophthalmitis about fivefold, from roughly 0.30% to 0.06%. It was added to standard care, not tested against no prophylaxis.

Study snapshot

Design
Prospective, randomized, partially masked, 2 × 2 factorial trial
Population
Patients having cataract surgery at 24 ophthalmology units in nine European countries
N
16,603 recruited; 16,211 in the intention-to-treat analysis
Interventions
Intracameral cefuroxime 1 mg in 0.1 mL normal saline at the end of surgery, and an intensive perioperative topical levofloxacin 0.5% regimen, each against none
Comparator
No intracameral antibiotic; placebo drops for the perioperative levofloxacin factor
Background care
All groups were prescribed topical levofloxacin 0.5% four times daily after surgery
Primary outcome
Postoperative endophthalmitis within 6 weeks, both clinically diagnosed (total) and microbiologically proven
Funding
Not verified

Effect

There were 29 cases of endophthalmitis, 20 of them microbiologically proven. Not having intracameral cefuroxime was associated with a 4.92-fold higher risk of total endophthalmitis (95% CI 1.87 to 12.9) after adjusting for other risk factors. The extra perioperative levofloxacin drops did not show significant protection.

From the pooled counts, that is about an 80% relative reduction and an absolute reduction of 0.23 percentage points, or about 427 operations to prevent one case at this trial's baseline risk. I calculated these from the counts above.

What this was not

This was not antibiotics versus no antibiotics. Everyone received postoperative levofloxacin drops, so the question was whether an intracameral dose adds protection on top of usual care. The trial did not compare cefuroxime with intracameral moxifloxacin, and it does not show that postoperative drops are unnecessary.

How far to trust it

Moderate for a substantial benefit; the exact size is imprecise

A large randomized trial with a consistent direction of effect. The rating stops at moderate because there were only 29 events, the confidence intervals are wide, the trial stopped early after a trend favoring cefuroxime, which can exaggerate benefit, and 392 of the recruited patients were not in the analysis.

What I'd change

Compare intracameral cefuroxime with intracameral moxifloxacin and with a no-drops arm under povidone-iodine antisepsis, with masked outcome assessment and a prespecified stopping rule, so the size of the benefit and the need for drops are both tested.

Clinical significance takeaway

A single intracameral cefuroxime dose at the end of cataract surgery substantially reduced a rare, potentially devastating infection. Intracameral means into the anterior chamber, so this is prophylaxis, distinct from intravitreal treatment of established endophthalmitis. I would use povidone-iodine antisepsis plus an intracameral antibiotic at the end of cataract surgery: cefuroxime 1 mg in 0.1 mL, from a sterile preparation, after checking allergy history. It supplements antisepsis.

Bottom line

ESCRS 2007: for cataract surgery, intracameral cefuroxime 1 mg at the end of the case cut endophthalmitis about fivefold (0.06% vs 0.30%, NNT about 430); give an intracameral antibiotic with povidone-iodine antisepsis, checking allergy history, and keep postoperative drops unless a trial shows they are unnecessary.
