EAGLE: early lens extraction for angle closure

In patients 50 or older with newly diagnosed angle-closure disease, clear-lens extraction left people on fewer drops and needing far fewer extra operations than laser iridotomy plus drops. It improved patient-reported health status, but it did not test whether lens extraction prevents blindness.

Study snapshot

Design
Multicenter, open-label randomized controlled trial; web-based randomization
Population
Age 50 or older, no cataract, newly diagnosed primary angle closure (PAC) with IOP of 30 mm Hg or higher, or primary angle-closure glaucoma (PACG). Recruited 2009 to 2011 at 30 hospital eye services in five countries
N
419 randomized: 208 to lens extraction and 211 to standard care. As reported, 155 had PAC and 263 had PACG
Intervention
Clear-lens extraction
Comparator
Standard care: laser peripheral iridotomy (LPI) plus topical medication, with further treatment added as needed
Co-primary outcomes
At 36 months: patient-reported health status (EQ-5D), IOP, and cost-effectiveness per quality-adjusted life year
Funding
Medical Research Council

Effect

Mean IOP was 1.18 mm Hg lower after lens extraction (95% CI −1.99 to −0.38). Health status on the EQ-5D was 0.052 higher (95% CI 0.015 to 0.088). The cost per quality-adjusted life year gained was £14,284 at three years.

In a later secondary analysis of the 369 patients who completed 36 months, 90% of the lens extraction group had IOP under 21 mm Hg without additional surgery, against 67% after standard care. Medication-free control was 66% vs 18% (odds ratio 10.1, 95% CI 6.1 to 16.8). This analysis was not part of the primary results.

Why a small IOP difference still matters

Both groups were escalated until pressure was acceptable. A 1.2 mm Hg gap in final IOP hides how much treatment each group needed to get there. The drop count and extra operations carry the real difference.

What this was not

This was not a trial of angle-closure suspects with uncomplicated narrow angles. It enrolled people with PAC and a high IOP or with PACG, so it does not support lens extraction for everyone with a narrow angle. It also did not show that lens extraction prevents more blindness: the outcomes were treatment burden, pressure and patient-reported health.

How far to trust it

Moderate for less treatment burden and better health status in eligible patients; low for blindness prevention

A pragmatic randomized trial with a clear direction across several outcomes, and a registered protocol. The rating stops at moderate because patients and clinicians could not be masked to surgery versus laser, the health-status outcome is self-reported, and 16% of patients lacked 3-year health-status data. The IOP difference is small, and vision loss was too rare to compare.

What I'd change

Follow patients past 3 years with visual field progression as a prespecified primary outcome, since blindness prevention is the question patients actually ask. Set escalation thresholds in advance so unmasked clinicians have less room to treat the two arms differently, and use a masked IOP assessor. A separate trial should test whether PAC suspects benefit at all.

Clinical significance takeaway

For a patient over 50 with PAC and an IOP of 30 mm Hg or higher, or with PACG, early lens extraction is a reasonable first-line option to discuss with glaucoma rather than starting automatically with laser iridotomy. Coverage of the 2026 AAO angle-closure Preferred Practice Pattern says it cites EAGLE for early lens extraction. I would not extend the result to angle-closure suspects. Acute angle closure is a separate emergency: lower the pressure promptly, then arrange definitive treatment. EAGLE excluded patients with a previous acute attack. Whichever route is chosen, gonioscopy, optic-nerve and visual field monitoring continue.

Bottom line

EAGLE 2016: for a patient 50 or older with newly diagnosed PAC and IOP of 30 mm Hg or higher, or PACG, clear-lens extraction needed fewer drops than laser iridotomy plus drops (0.4 vs 1.3 at 3 years); discuss early lens extraction with glaucoma, otherwise iridotomy with drops.
