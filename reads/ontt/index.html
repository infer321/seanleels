ONTT: corticosteroids for acute optic neuritis

IV methylprednisolone sped up visual recovery but did not improve 6-month acuity, and oral prednisone alone did nothing for vision while increasing new attacks of optic neuritis.

Study snapshot

Design
Multicenter, randomized, placebo-controlled, three arms
Population
Adults aged 18 to 46 with acute unilateral demyelinative optic neuritis, at 15 US centers
N
457 patients
Interventions
IV methylprednisolone 1 g/day (250 mg every 6 hours) for 3 days, then oral prednisone 1 mg/kg/day for 11 days; or oral prednisone 1 mg/kg/day for 14 days; each followed by a short taper
Comparator
Oral placebo for 14 days
Primary outcome
Contrast sensitivity and visual field. Visual acuity and color vision secondary
Follow-up
6 months in the main report; 1 year in the follow-up report
Funding
U.S. Public Health Service

Read with

  • 1-year follow-upBeck and Cleary, Arch Ophthalmol 1993Vision at 1 year was similar in all three arms; the benefit of IV steroids was short term.
  • SafetyChrousos et al., JAMA 1993Side effects of the steroid regimens, including the two serious events.
  • Later trialMorrow et al., JAMA Neurol 2018High-dose oral prednisone vs IV methylprednisolone in 55 patients with acute optic neuritis.

What they found

IV methylprednisolone vs placebo

Visual function recovered faster with IV methylprednisolone, particularly the reversal of visual-field defects (P = 0.0001). The differences narrowed over time. At 6 months the IV group had slightly better visual fields (P = 0.054), contrast sensitivity (P = 0.026) and color vision (P = 0.033), but not better visual acuity (P = 0.66).

At 1 year, acuity of 20/40 or better was reached by 95% on placebo, 94% after IV treatment and 91% on oral prednisone. None of the four measures of visual function differed significantly from placebo.

Oral prednisone alone

Oral prednisone at 1 mg/kg/day did not differ from placebo for recovery, and new episodes of optic neuritis in either eye were more common (relative risk 1.79, 95% CI 1.08 to 2.95). The IV group did not have more new episodes than placebo, but ONTT was not designed to show that IV treatment prevents them.

Safety

Minor effects were common in both steroid arms: sleep disturbance, mood change, stomach upset, facial flushing and weight gain. Two serious events occurred, both in the IV group and both fully reversible: psychotic depression in one patient and acute pancreatitis in another.

The later oral-dose trial

In 55 randomized patients (45 analyzed), IV methylprednisolone 1,000 mg/day was compared with oral prednisone 1,250 mg/day, both for 3 days. P100 latency on visual evoked potentials improved by 62.9 ms with IV and 66.7 ms with oral at 6 months (P = 0.07), and visual acuity did not differ significantly. Participants knew which they received.

This supports high-dose oral treatment as an alternative. It does not rescue the standard-dose oral regimen tested in ONTT, and "no significant difference" in a trial this small is not proof of equivalence.

How far to trust it

Moderate for faster recovery without a 6-month acuity benefit

A well-run randomized trial with standardized, reading-center–graded testing. The rating stops at moderate because the IV group was not masked, the primary outcomes were contrast sensitivity and visual field rather than acuity, and several 6-month differences were borderline.

The number that matters

1.79
Relative risk of new optic neuritis

Oral prednisone 1 mg/kg/day vs placebo (95% CI 1.08 to 2.95).

What I'd change

Make visual acuity a prespecified primary outcome and mask the IV arm with a placebo infusion, so the question patients care about, final vision, is tested directly and the fast-recovery benefit isn't open to expectation bias.

Clinical significance takeaway

If we choose steroids for this typical optic neuritis, I'd use methylprednisolone 1 g IV daily for three days, explaining that it speeds recovery rather than improving final acuity. Bioequivalent high-dose oral treatment is another option. I would not use prednisone 1 mg/kg/day alone.

Bottom line

ONTT 1992: for acute optic neuritis, IV methylprednisolone speeds recovery but not 6-month acuity; offer it if faster recovery matters (high-dose oral is a reasonable alternative), but avoid oral prednisone 1 mg/kg alone (recurrence RR 1.79).
