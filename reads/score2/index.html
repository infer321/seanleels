SCORE2: Avastin vs Eylea for central retinal vein occlusion

In macular edema from central or hemiretinal vein occlusion, monthly Avastin (bevacizumab) gained 18.6 letters and monthly Eylea (aflibercept) 18.9 at 6 months. Avastin met the noninferiority margin for vision.

Study snapshot

Design
Randomized noninferiority trial, 1:1,
Population
362 patients with macular edema from central or hemiretinal vein occlusion with central or hemiretinal vein occlusion; some had been treated before
Treatment
Avastin 1.25 mg (n = 182) or Eylea 2.0 mg (n = 180) intravitreal every 4 weeks through month 6
Primary outcome
Mean change in electronic ETDRS letter score from randomization to month 6. Noninferiority margin 5 letters, tested with a one-sided 97.5% confidence interval
Funding
Not verified

Effect

The mean gains were nearly identical. The lower bound of the confidence interval, −3.07 letters, stays inside the 5-letter margin, so a disadvantage larger than about 3 letters is unlikely. That supports similar clinical effectiveness, not merely a non-significant difference.

What this was not

Noninferiority at 6 months with monthly dosing does not show equivalence over years, with extended dosing, or in branch RVO. The trial also did not show similar safety: it was too small for rare events, and the abstract lists one culture-negative endophthalmitis in the Avastin group so similar adverse-event counts do not prove identical safety.

Who it applies to

The population mixed new and previously treated eyes, mostly CRVO, so the result is broadly useful for those patients but does not tell you how to treat BRVO.

How far to trust it

Highfor 6-month vision noninferiority with monthly dosing

A large, pragmatic-sized trial with an objective letter-score outcome, 96.1% completion and a prespecified margin. It is limited to 6 months and monthly dosing.

What I'd change

Follow patients beyond 6 months with a treat-and-extend arm, include branch RVO, and report anatomic outcomes and treatment burden as prespecified end points. A larger safety dataset would be needed to say anything firm about rare events.

Clinical significance takeaway

For vision loss from center-involving macular edema due to CRVO or HRVO, anti-VEGF is first-line, and monthly Avastin is an evidence-supported, lower-cost option that was noninferior to Eylea for vision at 6 months. Reassess VA and OCT at 4 weeks, watch for neovascularization, and consider switching if the response is poor. Steroids are generally second-line because of IOP elevation and cataract.

Bottom line

SCORE2 2017: for macular edema from CRVO or hemiretinal RVO, monthly Avastin (bevacizumab) was noninferior to Eylea (aflibercept) for vision at 6 months (+18.6 vs +18.9 letters); start with Avastin where acceptable, otherwise Eylea.
